# Repair Rewind — recorded 2:20 demo

This sequence describes the final 140-second edit of actual Lens Studio Preview recordings. The visible product label identifies the **simulated assembly and session memory**. Native input and camera movement were operated through the host UI. Selected passages from two takes omit idle time; no mockup or scripted test fixture replaces the Preview footage.

| Time | Actual preview action | Narration intent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:15 | Look down at assembled object and empty tray. | Taking something apart is easy; remembering where everything belongs after an interruption is harder. |
| 0:15–0:45 | Select cover, Remember & lift; repeat ring and gear. | Each Remember & lift action records the part's original position and sequence. Numbered breadcrumbs stay in the world. |
| 0:45–1:00 | Reassemble. Return gear and explicitly confirm it. | The latest recorded part goes back first. Arrival and user confirmation are separate. |
| 1:00–1:15 | Pause, then Resume on ring. | An interruption hides guidance; the same pending part returns when you resume. |
| 1:15–1:40 | Back once, replay and confirm the ring. Make a small Preview camera move. | A mistaken confirmation can be undone. The destination remains where it was recorded as the view moves. |
| 1:40–2:00 | Return/confirm cover. Show completion and confirmed Reset. | A complete loop, ready for another session. |
| 2:00–2:20 | Brief design reflection over the restored assembly. | Part, destination and order belong beside the task. This is a simulated assembly with session memory; device comfort, real-world marking and persistent anchors remain untested. |

The ring-return cut at 1:12 moves from a sample labelled “moving” to a later shot labelled “confirm placement”; it is an edit, not a continuous timing measurement. The underlying native tests separately check motion and confirmation behavior.

The final 1080 × 1920, 30 fps export contains 4,200 frames and presents for exactly 140 seconds. Full decoding and 21 sampled frames passed independent review. No desktop/account content was found in those samples. Narration and captions follow the edit; no human listening or frame-by-frame review is claimed. Public upload and final submission remain separate delivery actions.

The short camera move in the video supplements separate native acceptance checks for walkaround, translated/rotated parents, offscreen targets and negative input cases; it does not replace them.

Recording route: the native Preview toolbar's Record Video control, followed by its local save dialog. Snap documents this capability in [Previewing Your Lens](https://developers.snap.com/lens-studio/lens-studio-workflow/previewing-your-lens).
