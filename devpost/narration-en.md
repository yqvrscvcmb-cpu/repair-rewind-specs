# Repair Rewind — recorded English narration

This is the spoken text used in the 2:20 final edit of actual Lens Studio Preview footage, with pauses for actions. Local macOS Samantha narration and English captions accompany the native recordings. Technical and sampled visual review is documented in [verification](verification.md); subjective audio quality has not had a human listening review.

## Opening — 0:00–0:15

Taking something apart is easy. Remembering how it goes back together after an interruption is harder. Repair Rewind gives makers and repair learners a spatial memory of their own actions.

## Remember — 0:15–0:45

This is a simulated mechanical assembly in Lens Studio Preview. I select the cover and choose Remember and lift. The Lens records its original position, orientation and place in the sequence. The part moves into its own tray slot, while a numbered outline stays behind. I repeat that for the ring and gear. I decide what to record; this is not automatic object recognition.

## Reverse — 0:45–1:00

Reassemble starts with the last part I recorded. The gear and its destination light up together. Returning the model does not confirm the step. I confirm it explicitly.

## Interrupt — 1:00–1:15

Now I pause. The guidance disappears. When I resume, the ring is still next, and the original sequence is intact.

## Recover — 1:15–1:40

A mistaken confirmation should be recoverable. Back reopens exactly the last step and returns that part to the tray. I can replay it without recording everything again. As the view moves, the saved destination stays where it was recorded.

## Complete — 1:40–2:00

I return and confirm the cover. The session is complete. Reset asks for confirmation before clearing the observations and restoring the starting arrangement.

## Learning — 2:00–2:20

The design puts part, destination and order beside the task. Building it taught us to separate movement from confirmation, preserve world coordinates, and make interruptions reversible. This prototype remembers one Lens session. Real-world marking, long-term anchors and comfort on glasses still need testing.
